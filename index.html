Words carry journeys

Language, Tongue & Runes

Languages are related by historical descent, not simply by resemblance. The tree below is a compact reference; it complements the language maps above.

ᛘᛅᛚ — MÁL

Old Norse mál can mean speech, language or a matter of discussion.

ᛏᚢᚾᚴᛅ — TUNGA

Old Norse tunga means tongue and can also refer to a language.

These rune spellings are stylised approximations for display, not a universal ancient spelling system. The rune row and spelling conventions changed across time and place.

Two branches, one reconstructed ancestor

Proto-Indo-European (PIE) ├── Proto-Italic │ └── Latin │ └── Romance languages │ ├── Spanish │ ├── Portuguese │ ├── French │ ├── Italian │ └── Romanian │ └── Proto-Germanic └── Proto-Norse └── Old Norse ├── Icelandic ├── Norwegian ├── Danish ├── Swedish └── Faroese

This is simplified: Proto-Norse and Old Norse represent different historical stages, and the modern languages did not all descend from one identical Old Norse variety.

Which runes belong to which time?

Elder Futhark is associated with early Germanic inscriptions, broadly around the 2nd–8th centuries CE. During the Viking Age, Scandinavia predominantly used Younger Futhark. PIE itself is reconstructed by linguists: it had no attested runic or Latin alphabet of its own.

Evidence & imagination

History without losing the wonder

⚓ The historical sea roads

Viking-Age Scandinavian communities connected with the British Isles, the North Atlantic, the Baltic, and river networks reaching eastern Europe and the Black Sea. The routes varied by period, purpose and political context.

Saaremaa's Salme ship burials provide important archaeological evidence of early maritime contacts across the Baltic. They do not, by themselves, establish every voyage or route depicted in a fantasy map.

🌳 The living world tree

Tree, ship and compass make a useful visual language for Vikingers: ancestry, movement and orientation. They can connect Nordic myth with music, stories and other cultures without implying that all traditions share one historical origin.

Fantasy maps are welcome here. Their labels should simply make clear when a border, route, empire or spiritual connection is an artistic invention rather than an established historical fact.

Editorial principle: distinguish attested history, scholarly reconstruction, modern interpretation and fantasy. They can coexist on one site without being presented as the same kind of evidence.
